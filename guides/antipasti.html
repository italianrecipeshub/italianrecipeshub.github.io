Antipasti: How to Build an Italian Starter Board

Antipasti means before the meal, and in Italy that is a course in its own right. A good antipasto board is not a random pile of snacks. It is a balance of salty, sharp, creamy and fresh, with bread to tie it together. It is also the easiest impressive food you can serve, because almost nothing needs cooking.

What antipasti means

In Italy, antipasti open the meal and wake up the appetite. They can be as simple as bread and olives or as generous as a table full of cured meats, cheeses and marinated vegetables. The point is variety in small amounts, so guests can taste a little of everything.

Antipasti are usually served at room temperature, which makes them ideal for entertaining. You can prepare everything in advance, set it out, and enjoy your own party instead of standing at the stove.

Bruschetta and bread

Bruschetta is grilled bread rubbed with garlic and topped, most famously, with chopped tomato, basil and olive oil. Use a good country loaf, slice it thick, grill or toast it until crisp, and rub the cut side with a garlic clove while it is still hot.

Keep the topping simple and season it well. Other good toppings are white beans with sage, mushrooms with thyme, or ricotta with honey. Always serve bruschetta at once, because the bread softens quickly under a wet topping.

Cured meats and cheeses

Choose two or three cured meats rather than six. Prosciutto, salami and mortadella cover the range from delicate to rich. Fold or roll the slices loosely so they are easy to pick up, and let them come to room temperature for the best flavour.

For cheese, offer contrast: a soft cheese such as mozzarella or burrata, a semi-hard cheese such as pecorino, and a hard cheese such as Parmesan. Add a little honey or mostarda for the hard cheeses. About 30 grams of each cheese per person is a good guide.

Olives, vegetables and pickles

Olives, marinated artichokes, roasted peppers and pickled vegetables add sharpness and cut through the richness of meat and cheese. Buy good-quality olives and marinate them yourself with orange peel, fennel seeds and olive oil for an easy upgrade.

Roast peppers at home by charring them over a flame or under the grill, then peeling and dressing with oil and garlic. They keep for days in the fridge and taste better than anything from a jar.

Putting the board together

Start with the largest items, the cheeses, then add the meats, then fill the gaps with olives, vegetables and nuts. Leave space; a crowded board is hard to serve from. Add bread and breadsticks last, around the edges.

Plan on about 100 to 150 grams of food per person for a starter board, more if it is the main event. Serve with a light red wine or a spritzer, and keep the main course simple, because nobody wants a heavy meal after a generous antipasto.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much antipasti per person?

About 100 to 150 g of food per person as a starter, including meat, cheese and vegetables, plus bread.

What is the difference between antipasti and tapas?

Antipasti are Italian starters served before a meal, usually cold and built around cured meat, cheese and vegetables. Tapas are Spanish small plates, often hot, served as snacks or a meal.

Can I prepare antipasti the day before?

Most of it, yes. Marinate vegetables and olives, slice cheese, and store everything covered in the fridge. Slice bread and assemble the board on the day.
