Bruschetta: Grilled Bread, Tomato and Basil Done Right

Bruschetta is one of the simplest dishes in Italian cooking, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Soggy bread, watery tomatoes, and garlic that overpowers everything are the usual problems. Done right, bruschetta is crisp bread, ripe tomatoes, good olive oil, and basil, and it takes ten minutes. It is the perfect start to a summer meal.

The bread

Use a good country loaf or ciabatta, sliced about two centimetres thick. The bread should be sturdy enough to hold a topping without collapsing, but not so dense that it is hard to bite. Grill or toast the slices until golden and crisp on both sides, preferably over a flame or under a hot grill.

While the bread is still hot, rub one side with a cut clove of garlic. The heat opens the bread's surface and the garlic melts into it. This gives a gentle garlic flavour without raw garlic bite. Drizzle with olive oil straight away.

The tomato topping

Use ripe, flavourful tomatoes, preferably small ones such as cherry or vine tomatoes, chopped into small pieces. Season with salt and leave them for ten minutes so the salt draws out some juice. Drain off most of the liquid, or the bread will go soggy.

Tear in fresh basil leaves, add a drizzle of good olive oil, and taste for seasoning. The topping should be bright and fresh, not saucy. Some cooks add a little finely chopped red onion or a drop of balsamic vinegar, but the classic version needs nothing more than tomato, basil, oil, and salt.

Assembling and serving

Assemble bruschetta at the last moment, just before serving. Spoon the topping onto the garlic-rubbed side of the bread and serve immediately. If you top the bread too early, the juice soaks in and the crispness is lost.

Serve bruschetta as a starter or as part of an antipasti spread, with a glass of wine or a spritzer. It is best eaten with the hands, standing in the kitchen, while the rest of dinner cooks. That is the Italian way.

Variations to try

Once you have the classic version, try other toppings. White beans with sage and olive oil, mushrooms cooked with garlic and thyme, or ricotta with honey and black pepper are all excellent. Roasted peppers with capers, or aubergine with mint, make good summer versions.

Whatever the topping, the rules stay the same: crisp bread, a topping that is not too wet, and assembly at the last moment. Bruschetta is a technique as much as a recipe.

Bruschetta through the seasons

Bruschetta changes with the seasons, and that is part of its charm. In spring, try peas with mint and ricotta. In summer, the classic tomato and basil is unbeatable, when tomatoes are at their best. In autumn, mushrooms cooked with garlic and thyme are rich and warming. In winter, white beans with sage and olive oil are simple and satisfying.

The method stays the same whatever the topping: crisp grilled bread, a topping that is not too wet, and assembly at the last moment. Use what is in season and what you have, and let the topping be the star. Bruschetta is a way of cooking with the seasons, not a single recipe.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I stop bruschetta going soggy?

Salt and drain the tomatoes first, and assemble the bruschetta just before serving. Never top the bread in advance.

What bread is best for bruschetta?

A sturdy country loaf or ciabatta, sliced thick and grilled until crisp. Soft sandwich bread will collapse.

Should I use raw garlic on the bread?

Rub a cut clove over the hot toast. The heat softens the garlic flavour so it is gentle, not harsh.
